# ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Practitioner Pack

## What it is
The first certifiable international standard for an **AI Management System (AIMS)**. It covers organisations that develop, provide or use AI systems. It uses the same Harmonized Structure as ISO 27001, so clauses 4–10 integrate naturally with an existing ISMS. Certification runs the same way: Stage 1 and Stage 2, annual surveillance, a three-year cycle.

Related standards:
- ISO/IEC 22989: AI concepts and terminology
- ISO/IEC 23894: AI risk management guidance
- ISO/IEC 42005: AI system impact assessment guidance
- ISO/IEC 42006: requirements for bodies certifying AIMS

## Defining your role (do this first)
Clause 4 requires you to determine your role(s) relative to AI systems. Examples:
- **AI provider**: offers an AI product or service
- **AI producer or developer**: builds models or systems
- **AI customer or user**: uses third-party AI, e.g. an LLM API or SaaS AI features
- Partners, data providers, subjects

Most startups are both **providers** (AI features in their product) and **users** (internal AI tools, coding assistants). Scope and controls follow from these roles.

## Clauses (own words, with AI-specific deltas vs ISO 27001)
| Clause | AI-specific emphasis |
|---|---|
| 4 Context | Role determination; intended purpose of AI systems; climate change consideration in 4.1/4.2 |
| 5 Leadership | An **AI policy** (5.2); roles for AI oversight |
| 6 Planning | **AI risk assessment** (6.1.2), **AI risk treatment** with SoA against Annex A (6.1.3), and **AI system impact assessment** (6.1.4) on individuals, groups and society; AI objectives (6.2) |
| 7 Support | Competence in AI; awareness; documentation |
| 8 Operation | Run the risk assessments, treatment and impact assessments in practice (8.2–8.4) |
| 9–10 | Monitoring, internal audit, management review, improvement, the same as ISO 27001 |

## Annex A control objectives (38 controls across 9 areas)
| Area | Theme (own words) |
|---|---|
| A.2 | Policies related to AI: AI policy, alignment with other policies, review |
| A.3 | Internal organisation: roles, responsibilities, reporting concerns |
| A.4 | Resources for AI systems: documenting data, tooling, compute, system and human resources |
| A.5 | Assessing impacts of AI systems: an impact assessment process, documented results, effects on individuals, groups and society |
| A.6 | AI system life cycle: objectives for responsible development, design, verification and validation, deployment, operation and monitoring, technical documentation, event logs |
| A.7 | Data for AI systems: data acquisition, quality, provenance, preparation |
| A.8 | Information for interested parties: user documentation, external reporting, incident communication |
| A.9 | Use of AI systems: processes and objectives for responsible use, intended use |
| A.10 | Third-party and customer relationships: allocating responsibilities, suppliers, customers |

Supporting annexes:
- **Annex B**: implementation guidance
- **Annex C**: potential organisational objectives (fairness, transparency, security, safety, privacy, accountability and more) and risk sources
- **Annex D**: domains and sectors

## Minimum viable artefacts
- AIMS scope and AI system inventory (system, purpose, role, model/provider, data, users, risk level)
- AI policy
- AI risk assessment methodology and register
- AI system impact assessments (for systems that affect people)
- Statement of Applicability against Annex A
- Acceptable use rules for AI tools (staff use of LLMs)
- Supplier assessment for AI vendors (model providers, AI SaaS)
- Monitoring and incident process for AI (drift, misuse, harmful output)

## Crossovers
- **EU AI Act**: ISO 42001 isn't a harmonised standard under the Act, but it gives a governance backbone (risk management, documentation, human oversight, logging) that supports AI Act obligations.
- **GDPR**: AI processing personal data usually needs a DPIA (Art. 35). Automated decisions engage Art. 22 (EU) / Arts. 22A–22D (UK GDPR, as amended by the DUAA 2025).
- **ISO 27001**: shared clauses 4–10. Integrate as one management system with two scopes.
