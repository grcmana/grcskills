# ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Practitioner Pack

## What it is
An international standard for an **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**. Certification is by an accredited certification body (CB) via a two-stage audit, then annual surveillance audits and a recertification audit every three years.

- Stage 1: documentation and readiness review (is the ISMS designed and in place?)
- Stage 2: implementation audit (is it operating effectively?)
- Transition from the 2013 edition ended on 2025-10-31. Any certificate still referencing 2013 is no longer valid.
- Amendment 1 (2024) added climate change considerations to clauses 4.1 and 4.2. Organisations must consider whether climate change is a relevant issue and whether interested parties have related requirements.

## Structure
- **Clauses 4–10**: the management system requirements, which are mandatory. They follow the ISO Harmonized Structure shared with ISO 9001, ISO 42001 and others.
- **Annex A**: a reference set of 93 controls in 4 themes. You select controls through risk treatment and justify them in the Statement of Applicability (SoA).
  - A.5 Organizational: 37 controls
  - A.6 People: 8 controls
  - A.7 Physical: 14 controls
  - A.8 Technological: 34 controls
- ISO/IEC 27002:2022 gives implementation guidance for each Annex A control. It isn't certifiable.

## Clauses at a glance (own words)
| Clause | What the auditor wants to see |
|---|---|
| 4 Context | Internal/external issues, interested parties and their requirements, a defined and justified ISMS scope, and how the ISMS is established and maintained |
| 5 Leadership | Visible top-management commitment, an information security policy, assigned roles and responsibilities |
| 6 Planning | A risk assessment method and results, risk treatment plan, SoA, measurable security objectives with plans to achieve them, and planned changes (6.3) |
| 7 Support | Resources, competence (with evidence), awareness, communication, and control of documented information |
| 8 Operation | Operational planning and control, risk assessments run at planned intervals or on significant change, risk treatment implemented |
| 9 Performance evaluation | Monitoring and measurement, an internal audit programme, management review |
| 10 Improvement | Continual improvement, nonconformity handling and corrective action |

## Mandatory documented information (minimum the CB will ask for)
- ISMS scope (4.3)
- Information security policy (5.2)
- Risk assessment process (6.1.2) and risk treatment process (6.1.3)
- Statement of Applicability (6.1.3 d)
- Risk treatment plan (6.1.3 e / 6.2)
- Information security objectives (6.2)
- Evidence of competence (7.2)
- Documented information needed for operational control (8.1)
- Results of risk assessments (8.2) and risk treatment (8.3)
- Monitoring and measurement results (9.1)
- Internal audit programme and results (9.2)
- Management review results (9.3)
- Nonconformities and corrective actions (10.2)

Annex A controls also imply documents. Common ones are an access control policy, an asset inventory, an incident management procedure, supplier security requirements, a business continuity / ICT readiness plan, and secure development rules.

## Controls startups most often get challenged on
- **A.5.9 / A.5.10**: an asset inventory and acceptable use that actually match reality
- **A.5.15–A.5.18**: access control, identity management, authentication, access reviews (show the review records)
- **A.5.19–A.5.23**: supplier security, including **A.5.23 cloud services**
- **A.5.24–A.5.28**: incident management, from planning to evidence collection (has it been exercised?)
- **A.5.29 / A.5.30**: security during disruption and ICT readiness for business continuity
- **A.5.7**: threat intelligence (new in 2022; keep it proportionate)
- **A.6.3**: awareness and training records
- **A.8.8**: technical vulnerability management (SLAs and evidence of patching)
- **A.8.9**: configuration management (new in 2022)
- **A.8.12**: data leakage prevention (new in 2022; proportionate approach)
- **A.8.15 / A.8.16**: logging and monitoring
- **A.8.25–A.8.29**: secure development lifecycle and security testing
- **A.8.32**: change management

## Common failure points
- A scope that excludes the actual product or cloud environment
- An SoA that says "implemented" with nothing to show for it
- A risk register with no owners, no treatment dates, or never reviewed
- No internal audit, or an internal audit performed by the person who built the ISMS (independence)
- Management review held as a ritual with no minuted inputs or outputs as required by 9.3
- Policies with no approval date, owner or review cycle

## Typical timeline (startup, 20–150 staff)
3–6 months to Stage 1 readiness when there's a dedicated owner. Stage 1 to Stage 2 is usually 4–12 weeks.
